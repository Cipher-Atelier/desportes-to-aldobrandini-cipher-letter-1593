## Why r36 is not decided

The two admitted A/N forms (N in EN and NOM) retain open upper/lower-right apertures and distinguishable boundaries. Their dark central crossings do not create a solid filled body comparable to the dense final r36 mark [1057,4542,1100,4635]. No blur, thresholding, filling or generated stroke was used to manufacture an equally inked A. Clean/open A/N values are useful contextual examples, not the matched F/A discriminator.

The admitted E/R in EN has loops touching its descending stem. There is no unambiguously detached oblique component whose group membership is secured by positional alignment. It cannot decide r36's short stroke [999,4540,1057,4635].

NOM gives a contextual O-labelled B/O with visible loops/stem, but no admitted H/U counterpart. The corrected UNE location has a provisional B-like first figure whose stem is less clear; its uncertain class cannot be promoted to a U-labelled H variation. Neither resemblance nor common historical attribution certifies identical handwriting or excludes a shortened/overlapped B in r36 [910,4524,973,4630]. BIENS/VIES and four/five-unit segmentation are neither confirmed nor refuted by this bounded test.
